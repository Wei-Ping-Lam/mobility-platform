# Two-Minute Demo Script

## 0:00-0:15 | Portfolio Overview

"Hosting a major event means moving thousands of people without overwhelming roads, transit, or the environment.

Mobility Readiness helps cities turn that challenge into practical decisions, using FIFA World Cup 2026 and all eleven U.S. host cities as our case study."

## 0:15-0:35 | Readiness Chart and Heatmap

"We start with the big picture: how do the host cities compare?

The readiness scores bring together venue access, traffic management, heat safety, and venue support. The heatmap reveals what drives each result, so decision-makers can look beyond a ranking and identify where action is needed."

## 0:35-0:55 | Visitor Movement > Peak Timing

"Next, we model when visitors arrive and leave.

These curves highlight concentrated demand around kickoff and after the match. The shaded bands show different attendance scenarios, making uncertainty visible rather than presenting one estimate as a guaranteed forecast.

That helps cities think about when additional service matters most."

## 0:55-1:10 | Venue Access

"Venue Access connects those demands to conditions on the ground.

Atlanta illustrates an important distinction: strong access does not automatically mean strong sustainability. We separate those scores and show the transit, walking, parking, and fleet evidence behind them."

## 1:10-1:40 | Dallas > Transit Solution

"Now let's turn that evidence into an action plan for Dallas.

The recommendation is to pre-position electric overflow buses, replacing equivalent conventional trips.

Under this planning scenario, that addresses approximately 540 passengers per match, avoids about 1.2 tonnes of operating CO2e, and costs roughly fourteen thousand dollars.

Notice that vehicle-miles saved stays at zero: replacing a bus's power source reduces emissions, but does not eliminate its trip."

## 1:40-2:00 | Capital Alternative, Then Traffic Controls

"For longer-term decisions, we also estimate capital costs and potential passenger capacity, accounting for reserve vehicles and event-day delays.

The traffic view maps documented closures in red.

These are transparent planning scenarios, not promises. Our goal is to help cities compare tradeoffs, prioritize sustainable actions, and build improvements that last beyond one tournament."
